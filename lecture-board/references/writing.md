# Writing: Say and notes

Say and the Why? and Caution notes are mathematical prose. If the project or the user has writing rules of their own, those take precedence; otherwise these apply.

## Mathematical register
Write like a good classical textbook (Akhiezer–Glazman, Kolmogorov–Fomin), following Halmos's "How to Write Mathematics": "Let…", "Denote…", "We call … if …", "Then…". Short and precise, no colloquialisms, no preamble about what comes next.

- A term is introduced once, at the moment it is first needed, by a one-sentence definition. Only standard mathematical terms, no ad hoc words. An informal word of the source is defined before use and attributed.
- A section, a chain of lemmas or a long proof opens with one sentence saying what question it answers or why the object is needed: the obstruction, or the example that forces the definition.
- Every sentence gives a definition, a statement, a reason or an action. A sentence whose deletion loses none of these is deleted. Between a motivation and what it leads to, write the action that connects them, naming the operation and the object: "We write out the Cayley table", "We apply Lemma 2 to $f-g$".
- Definition first, metaphor after; a metaphor never replaces a definition. Where a formula carries a statement, do not paraphrase it.
- A letter does not denote two objects in one talk. If a letter is taken, take another rather than add a disclaimer.
- No careless metaphors, no decorative glosses ("playing the role of…"), no long sentences with nested "since", "that is", "which".

## Prose
Work as an editor who prepares serious nonfiction for print (Gowers, "The Complete Plain Words"; Orwell, "Politics and the English Language"): no jargon, padding, stale metaphors or ready-made phrases, and no simplifying of a difficult thought. Read every sentence as someone who does not know the context, and read it aloud.

- A sentence has one reading. Each "it", "they", "which" points to one word.
- "This", "such", "the same" refer to something the text has named.
- Things are called by words the language has; no new phrases or metaphors coined for brevity.
- A sentence is grammatically complete. A verb may be left out only when the same verb stands next to it.
- Every word works, but the sentence is not cut down to a telegram.
- A word or thought repeated in neighbouring sentences goes unless the repetition does something. A term stays the same word throughout.
- A verb beats a nominalization ("the implementation of the evaluation of…").
- An impersonal or passive construction is rewritten when otherwise the reader cannot tell who acts.
- The logic between sentences is shown by connectives, chosen by the place in either language. The same connective does not stand in neighbouring sentences or captions; a short reason often goes first, in the same sentence, so that none is needed. Examples, not a closed list: «поэтому», «значит», «следовательно», «таким образом», «так как …, то», «поскольку», «ввиду», «в силу», «благодаря», «согласно», «отсюда следует», «это означает», «оказывается», «остаётся учесть», «остаётся воспользоваться», «имеем», «действительно», «кроме того», «помимо этого», «мы приходим к следующему»; "so", "hence", "thus", "therefore", "consequently", "it follows that", "since", "because", "in view of", "by", "this means that", "it remains to use", "indeed", "moreover", "in addition", "we arrive at the following".
- A colon stands only before a quotation, before a list announced by a general word, or before a part that unfolds the first. A colon that glues two sentences becomes a full stop, a dash or a conjunction.
- No overstatement ("crucial") and no overselling ("an elegant solution").
- Each correction is reread as a new sentence by the same points.

## Russian texts
- The register formulas are «Пусть…», «Обозначим…», «Назовём…, если…», «Тогда…», and the connecting actions «Выпишем таблицу Кэли», «Применим лемму 2 к $f-g$».
- The editor follows Нора Галь («Слово живое и мёртвое») and Чуковский («Живой как жизнь») against канцелярит, calques and stock phrases, and Розенталь for grammar and collocation: «является», «осуществляет», «данный» and chains of genitives give way to plain words.
- No anglicisms or calques where a Russian term exists. Foreign surnames keep their original spelling.
- A Why? may open with «Потому что…».
- Russian agent prose overuses the colon, so the colon rule applies strictly.
